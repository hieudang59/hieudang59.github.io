Good work needs room to happen. When every quiet moment gets filled with a notification or a new tab, it is hard to stay with a question long enough to understand it.

## Start with one clear task

Before opening a project, write down the next useful thing you can finish. A small, concrete goal is easier to return to when the day gets noisy.

I try to make the task specific enough to begin immediately: read one failing test, sketch one alternative, or simplify one confusing function.

## Protect a little space

Deep work does not need a perfect schedule. Even a short stretch without interruptions can help. I silence notifications, keep only the relevant files open, and leave a note about where to pick up before taking a break.

The note is the simplest part, and often the most helpful. It turns restarting into continuing.

## Reflect, then adjust

At the end of a focused session, take a moment to notice what helped and what got in the way. Keep the habits that worked; change one thing at a time.

The goal is not to optimize every minute. It is to make it a little easier to do work you care about.
